# Tuning grab points (by hand)

When something is put straight into a hand, it's held the same way every time.
That happens when you pick **Grab** from an object's context menu, and when you
pull a file out of a drawer. The pose it's held in comes from two transforms,
and both are meant to be tuned by people in a headset, not by code:

1. **The object's grab point**: where on the object a hand holds it.
2. **The hand's hold frame**: where in the hand that grab point goes.

The object ends up at `hand * hold_frame * grab_point⁻¹`, so the grab point
lands exactly on the hold frame (`Hand.hold_offset_for()` in
`scripts/player/hand.gd`).

Ordinary grabbing (closing your hand near something) is **not** affected. It
keeps whatever grip you took.

## See them: `--show-grab-points`

Run with the flag and every grabbable object shows an axis gizmo at its grab
point, and each hand shows one at its hold frame:

```
godot -- --show-grab-points                    # VR (or desktop without a headset)
godot -- --host --desktop --show-grab-points   # straight into your office, desktop
```

Axes: **X red, Y green, Z blue**, drawn on top of everything. When an object
is held properly, its gizmo and the hand's gizmo coincide.

## The convention

For both transforms:

- **+Y runs along the fingers** (from the wrist toward the fingertips).
- **+Z points out of the palm** (away from the hand, toward what the palm faces).
- **+X** follows from those (right-handed).

So an object's grab point says "put this spot in the palm, with this direction
along my fingers and this direction facing out of my palm".

## 1. An object's grab point

Each entity scene can have a `Marker3D` named **`GrabPoint`** as a direct child
of the root (`NetBody.grab_point()` in `scripts/entities/net_body.gd`). If
there's none, the object is held by its centre with its own axes.

Current ones:

| Scene | GrabPoint | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| `scenes/entities/props/chair.tscn` | (0, 0.48, 0.2) | top of the backrest |
| `scenes/entities/props/table.tscn` | (0, 0.35, 0.4) | middle of a long edge |
| `scenes/entities/props/lamp.tscn` | (0, 0.1, 0) | the pole |
| `scenes/entities/props/plant.tscn` | (0, 0.05, 0) | the pot |
| `scenes/entities/props/monitor.tscn` | (0, 0.14, -0.04) | the stand |
| `scenes/entities/props/drawer.tscn` | (0, 0.36, 0.25) | top front edge |

Documents and clipboards have none, so they're held by the middle, face out.

To tune one:

1. Open the scene in the Godot editor and select `GrabPoint`. Add one (a
   `Marker3D` child of the root, named exactly `GrabPoint`) if it's missing.
2. **Move** it to the spot a hand should close around.
3. **Rotate** it so green (+Y) points the way your fingers should run along
   the object, and blue (+Z) points the way your palm should face.
   - For a chair held by the back, that's green up and blue toward the seat's
     front.
   - To hold a flat thing (a document) face-up in an open palm, blue should
     point along the object's front.
4. Run with `--show-grab-points`. Use Grab from the object's menu and check
   how it sits: in the hand, the right way round, not through your arm.
   Repeat.

`monitor.tscn` and `drawer.tscn` are generated by
`tools/gen_widget_scenes.py`. If you regenerate them, edit the `GrabPoint`
lines there too, or your editor changes will be overwritten.

## 2. The hand's hold frames

Three constants near `hold_frame()` in `scripts/player/hand.gd`, one per way a
hand is driven:

- **`HOLD_TRACKED`** (optical hand tracking): relative to the **palm joint**,
  which has -Z toward the fingers and +Y out of the back of the hand. The
  default puts the hold point 5 cm off the palm on the palm side, 2 cm toward
  the fingers.
- **`HOLD_CONTROLLER`** (controllers): relative to the **grip pose**, whose
  origin is the middle of your closed fist. The default holds things in the
  fist, slightly forward.
- **`HOLD_DESKTOP`** (desktop): relative to the floating desktop hand, which
  faces the camera.

Each is `Transform3D(Basis(x, y, z), origin)`, where `x`, `y`, `z` are the hold
frame's axes written in the hand's own axes. That makes it easy to reason
about: "+Y along the fingers" for a palm joint (fingers are -Z) is
`y = Vector3(0, 0, -1)`, and "+Z out of the palm" (palm is -Y) is
`z = Vector3(0, -1, 0)`. Then `x = y × z`.

Runtimes differ slightly in joint and grip conventions, so check on every
headset you care about. The left and right hands use the same frame, so if an
object sits well in one hand and mirrored-wrong in the other, flip X for the
left hand in `hold_frame()` (`index == 0`).

## Checklist when something looks wrong

- **Held outside the hand, or through the back of it:** the hold frame's
  origin is on the wrong side. Change the sign of `HOLD_*`'s Y (tracked) or X
  (controller) offset.
- **Held backwards or upside down:** the object's `GrabPoint` rotation is off.
  Rotate it 180° about Y or Z in the editor.
- **Right idea but too far from the palm:** shorten the hold frame's offset,
  or move the `GrabPoint` toward the surface you grip.
- **Only wrong with controllers (or only with hands):** tune that `HOLD_*`
  constant only.
